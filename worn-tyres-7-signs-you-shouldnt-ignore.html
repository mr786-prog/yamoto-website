Worn Tyres? 7 Signs You Shouldn’t Ignore

Cracks, bulges, worn tread? Learn 7 signs your car tyres need replacing, with vital safety advice specifically for Hyderabad road conditions.

By Yamoto Technical Team
Published in Narsingi, Hyderabad
Reading time: 4 min read

Your tyres are the only part of your car touching the road. Every acceleration, emergency stop, and sharp turn relies entirely on four palm-sized contact patches. Worn tyres severely compromise braking distances, steering precision, and grip — especially during sudden downpours on Hyderabad’s fast-moving Outer Ring Road (ORR) and flyovers.

Here is what to look out for during your monthly vehicle check:

7 Signs It’s Time to Replace Your Car Tyres

1

Worn Tread

Check Tread Depth

Look for small raised bars molded inside the bottom of the tyre grooves — these are called tread wear indicators. When your tyre’s outer tread rubber has worn down level with these bars (about 1.6 mm), the tyre is legally and functionally finished.

Local Tip: While 1.6 mm is the legal minimum, stopping performance drops drastically below 3 mm in rainy monsoon conditions. When driving on wet stretches in Gachibowli, Kokapet, or HITEC City, shallow tread cannot disperse surface water, leading to terrifying aquaplaning.
2

Cracks on the Sidewall

Rubber Aging

Fine hairline cracks or "crazing" on the side of the tyre mean the rubber compounds are drying out and losing their elasticity, typically triggered by intense summer heat, direct sunlight, and age.

Hyderabad Roads Context: Hyderabad’s scorching summer temperatures often bake road tarmac past 50°C. Brittle rubber loses its structural integrity and can rupture without warning when subjected to highway speeds or sudden expansion joint impacts on the ORR.
3

Bulges or Bubbles

Immediate Danger

A visible bump, egg-shaped bulge, or bubble on the sidewall indicates that the tyre’s internal fabric plies and steel belts have torn or snapped, usually after taking a hard hit against a deep pothole or kerb.

⚠ Stop driving on it immediately. The only thing holding air inside is a thin layer of outer rubber. A bulge can burst catastrophic blowout at any moment. Fit your spare wheel or call Yamoto right away.

4

Constant Vibration

Alignment / Balance Issue

Shaking or fluttering through the steering wheel or car floor at speeds above 60 km/h is a warning sign. It often points to an unbalanced wheel assembly, but it can also be caused by an unevenly worn tyre, internal belt separation, or an oval-shaped flat spot.

Next Step: Have your wheels balanced and checked on our precision ATS ELGI 3D wheel alignment equipment in Narsingi to pinpoint whether a simple rebalancing will fix it or if the tyre core has warped.
5

Frequent Pressure Loss

Leak Warning

If one tyre continually triggers your TPMS light or needs air every week, you are dealing with more than routine pressure fluctuation. It could be a slow puncture from construction debris, a cracked valve stem, wheel rim bead corrosion, or an old puncture plug that has begun to seep.

Tyres with multiple patches or repairs near the outer shoulder cannot safely hold pressure and must be replaced.

6

Tyre Age (The 5-Year Rule)

Expiry Check

Find the four-digit DOT date code stamped in an oval on the tyre sidewall. For example, 2423 denotes manufacture during the 24th week of 2023.

Tyres naturally oxidize and degrade chemically over time, even on low-mileage city cars parked in shaded basements. Regardless of how deep the remaining tread looks, tyres older than 5 years should be professionally examined, and tyres past 7 to 10 years must be retired.

7

Poor Grip in the Rain

Braking Safety

If your car triggers its ABS frequently, fish-tails slightly on wet flyover ramps, or takes noticeably longer to come to a halt in drizzle, your tyre compound has hardened or the tread channels can no longer siphon water away.

Safety First: Wet braking distances for worn tyres can be more than double those of fresh rubber. Don't wait until an emergency braking scenario to find out your tyres have lost their grip.
